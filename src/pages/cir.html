01 · Research objective

Reach the same capability for substantially less total cost.

minimize  TotalCost(CIR, Q) / TotalCost(Strong Transformer, Q)

Q is matched capability. Both systems must reach the same level before costs are compared.

The primary metric is cost to capability. FLOPs, parameters, BPB, tokens per second and wall clock speed are inputs, not the target.

Every new claim must also be compared with the cheapest fair Transformer we know. We report two denominators side by side.

What total cost can include

  • Training compute and wall clock time
  • Hardware utilization
  • Memory, activations and optimizer state
  • Data exposure
  • Infrastructure, energy and amortized hardware

Two denominators

  • Standard. A strong Transformer with fair, generic improvements.
  • Matched modules. The same Transformer given every generic module CIR uses.
  • The second isolates what is specific to CIR.
{{tagMeasured}}
Read directly from an instrument, such as BPB or time per update.
{{tagEstimated}}
Computed from measurements, such as a cost ratio at matched Q.
{{tagProjected}}
Extrapolated beyond what was run. Never mixed with the other two.

03 · Current research state

What is being compared, and on what.

Implementation details beyond this level stay private while the research is active.

  • Program phase

    CIR's own mixers are closed for language modeling at this scale. The main line is a generic counted organization.

  • Counted organization

    A small one attention Transformer and counted n gram tables. Also a context cache, a longest match pointer and a tiny gate.

  • Cheapest configuration

    A039. 0.82M parameters, trained on 1,024 token chunks. {{tagMeasured}}

  • Target quality

    TF LGN. A local global Transformer with n gram heads, at its final quality.

  • Cheapest Transformer

    B1A. A Transformer with a single global attention layer, used for equal budget comparisons.

  • Quality metric

    Bits per token, scored only where the 12 token context never appears in training.

  • Data

    English, 3.86M tokens, and Indonesian, 31.2M tokens. Context of 4,096 tokens.

  • Hardware

    One commodity laptop CPU, Intel Core i5 13420H, two physical cores. No GPU yet.

  • Seeds

    Two seeds for the main counted results. Per language and ladder results use one.

  • Training cost to TF LGN quality

    Average 0.062 to 0.063×. Indonesian 0.032×. English 0.149× at best. {{tagEstimated}}

  • Inference cost

    Prefill 0.48 to 0.49× TF LGN. Decoding not measured. Count tables need about 434 MB. {{tagEstimated}}

  • CIR mixers against B1A

    A010 1.23 to 1.27×. A025, the cheapest hybrid, 0.949 to 0.985×. {{tagEstimated}}

04 · Current claim

Current claim

Scope: networks up to 1.4M parameters, 4,096 token context, one laptop CPU. Snapshot of {{cirAsOf}}.

CIR's own architectures have no cost advantage over the cheapest fair Transformer. The delta hybrid family breaks even at best.

A generic organization does show one. A small Transformer with counted n gram statistics reaches TF LGN quality cheaply.

Averaged over two languages, it needs 0.062 to 0.063× of TF LGN's training cost. This holds on two seeds.

The average hides a gap. Indonesian reaches 0.032×. English reaches 0.149× at best, and the gain fades as English data repeats.

This is not a new method. Close prior art exists, including modded nanogpt PR 380. Our contribution is attacked, reproducible cost accounting.

The breakthrough gate holds only on average and in Indonesian, at small scale. It is not established in English, at scale, or for CIR's own architecture.

06 · Cost to capability

A curve, not a headline number.

An advantage at one quality level, budget or language can vanish at another. We report each separately.

TotalCostRatio(Q) ≈ ρ_u × ρ_c

  • ρ_u

    Relative updates, or tokens, needed to reach matched Q.

  • ρ_c

    Relative cost per update, measured on one canonical instrument.

  • Counted statistics

    Counting the tables costs about 0.1 to 0.2% of training. Their lookup cost is included at inference.

07 · Baseline adversary

We keep making the Transformer stronger.

A baseline lane acts as an adversary. Several claims weakened or disappeared as a result. The counted claim was attacked the same way.

  • Optimizer: Muon for both sides

    The pure recurrent advantage disappeared. The Transformer won by 0.051 BPB.

    Claim withdrawn

  • Gated blocks for the Transformer

    The strongest Transformer in quality. A010 still held at 0.59 to 0.63.

    Claim narrowed

  • Local global attention

    9% cheaper per token at nearly equal quality. Early Q ratios rose to 0.78 to 0.82.

    Claim narrowed

  • Learning rate screening for baselines

    An inherited baseline learning rate was flagged. Dependent claims are marked contested until attacked.

    Rule added

  • Engram memory module

    Helped the Transformer more than A010, closing about 63% of the margin.

    Claim weakened

  • Static n gram heads

    With the same heads on both sides, the recurrent specific edge fell to about zero.

    Claim weakened

  • One global attention layer (B1A)

    Matched the previous baseline at half the cost per token. BPB claims were lost.

    Claim lost

  • Wider B1A at equal compute

    BPB tied with A010. Recall was slightly worse.

    Tie

  • Windowed B1A

    Measured at only 8.6% cheaper per update, with a small quality loss. B1A stays the frontier.

    Minor threat

  • Cheapest hybrid against B1A

    A025 broke even at 0.985×, or 0.949× optimized. Both frozen criteria failed.

    Family closed

  • Counted claim: n gram heads, larger models, longer training

    TF LGN, Transformers up to width 448 and a longer trained B1A all cost more.

    Claim held

  • Counted claim: the same short context trick

    TF LGN trained on short chunks lost quality and never reached the target.

    Claim held

  • Direct cost measurement of TF LGN

    The bridged estimate was about 5% low. Every ratio became less favourable.

    Claim corrected

  • Per language scoring and longer budgets

    English loses its advantage at 2× and 4× budget. Larger Transformers above 4× are unattacked.

    Claim narrowed
08 · Reduced uncertainty

What We Have Ruled Out

Each item below closed a branch of the search. That makes the next experiment cheaper to choose.

Architecture

Pure recurrence under a strong optimizer

A delta rule model loses to a Muon trained Transformer at this scale.

Architecture

Longer context as the route to 0.50

Moving from 4,096 to 8,192 tokens made A010's per token ratio worse.

Architecture

Static lookup as recall

Lookup tables cannot perform in context associative recall. A softmax layer is still needed.

Architecture

Several hybrid variants

Local selection, a surprisal cache and a per head hybrid layer each failed their gates.

Architecture

Recurrent state replacing distant attention

When attention was windowed, A010 lost as much quality as the Transformer. Its state carried almost no distant copies.

Analysis

Mixer substitution reaching 0.50 here

Replace attention with a free recurrent core. Canonical timings still leave a ratio of about 0.77 to 0.87. {{tagEstimated}}

Architecture

Thin recurrent hybrids against B1A

A025 kept part of A010's learning advantage but only broke even on cost.

Analysis

Training rules specific to recurrence

Every training rule lever found also applies to Transformers. None is specific to CIR here.

Capability

Reliable state tracking from sparse data

With 6% parity examples, the capability emerged in only one of three runs.

Capability

Cheap state tracking

A one layer Transformer with written steps reached the permutation target more cheaply.

Learning

Formal language warm up at 4M

A short warm up before text raised BPB. It hurt the CIR hybrid twice as much.

Organization

Counts inside the training loss

Training the network jointly with the counts failed. Mixing at inference works.

Organization

One gate for every language

A gate tuned on one language was worse than fixed weights on the other.

Organization

Smaller networks, pruned tables, first layer attention

Below 0.82M parameters, pruning rare counts or moving attention first all made results worse.

Method

Averages across languages

An average hid a large English gap. Claims are now reported per language.

Method

Ranking architectures at 250 updates

Rank correlation with final results was 0.09. Discovery runs now last 750 updates.

Method

Uncontrolled timing

Sessions with unpinned cores or background load are quarantined and remeasured.

Method

Endpoint cost for capability

Charging a plateaued baseline its full budget inflated CIR's advantage. It was corrected.

09 · Capability evaluation

Lower BPB is not general intelligence.

BPB measures text compression. Models with equal BPB can differ in recall, copying and state tracking. At 4M parameters, many capabilities cannot be measured at all.

10 · Scaling status

Small scale results do not prove large scale behavior.

Counted networks stay below 1.4M parameters and CIR mixers below 7M. Evidence, extrapolation and hypothesis are kept apart.

Evidence {{tagEstimated}}

Budget ladder

Against B1A at equal budget, the counted average stays at 0.048 to 0.067×. That holds from 0.5× to 4×. Indonesian stays near 0.04×.

Evidence {{tagEstimated}}

English does not hold

English moves from 0.053× to 0.121×. At 2× and 4× the target is out of reach.

Confound

Data repetition

The English corpus is seen 3.2 times at 1× and 12.7 times at 4×. Indonesian is seen far less. R161 tests this.

Extrapolation {{tagProjected}}

A fitted multiplier

A loss curve fit suggests about 15× equivalent compute. Part of it reflects the fixed size opponent's limit.

Evidence {{tagEstimated}}

CIR mixers across widths

Against B1A, A010's update advantage stayed at 0.70 to 0.71 from 4M to 7M. Its cost stayed above 1.

Unknown

Not yet tested

Larger networks, larger Transformers above 4× budget, bigger English data, GPUs. These exceed the current CPU budget.

11 · Current threats to the claim

Why the remaining advantage could disappear.

Some of these threats have already materialized. They are listed in order of current weight.

  • 01Language and data repetition

    The English gain is small and fades as data repeats. The cause is under test.

    Partly materialized

  • 02Scale

    Networks stay below 1.4M parameters. Larger Transformers may catch up above 4× budget.

    Open

  • 03Prior art

    The counted organization matches published work, including modded nanogpt PR 380.

    Acknowledged

  • 04Deployment cost

    Decoding is unmeasured and the count tables need about 434 MB.

    Open

  • 05The gate needs domain text

    About 4,000 tokens of text from the target domain are needed. A gate does not transfer across languages.

    Limits use

  • 06Same tools for the Transformer

    Given the same counted tools, a Transformer may match it. No D2 claim is made.

    Not claimed

  • 07CIR specific architecture

    Mixer substitution is bounded analytically, and no CIR specific candidate remains.

    Materialized

  • 08Hardware specificity

    One laptop CPU. GPU behavior may differ.

    Open

14 · Methodology

How CIR evaluates a candidate.

Hypotheses

Hypothesis led, with credences

Each experiment tests a named hypothesis. Beliefs are updated before and after.

Criteria

Frozen success and failure criteria

Pass conditions and predictions are written down before any result is seen.

Funnel

Discovery separate from confirmation

Discovery runs are short. Confirmation needs a full run, a second seed and a baseline attack.

Baselines

Adversarial baseline strengthening

A dedicated lane tries to make the Transformer beat CIR.

Mechanism

Mechanism before optimization

Probes explain why a result happens before it is built upon.

Fidelity

Multi fidelity experiments

Analysis and evaluation only probes come first. Full training runs come last.

Cost

Canonical cost accounting

Fixed cores, calibration and validity checks. Invalid sessions are remeasured.

Capability

Capability probes beyond BPB

Copying, recall, bracket closing and position use are checked on the same checkpoints.

Search

First principles architecture search

Candidates are derived from bounds and primitives before literature is consulted.

Prior art

Adversarial prior art review

Every idea is searched against published work. Rediscoveries are labeled as such.

Red team

Periodic knowledge red teaming

Accepted conclusions are attacked on a schedule. Errors found are logged and corrected.

Status

Epistemic labels

Knowledge carries a status, such as supported, contested, superseded or quarantined.

15 · Limitations

What these results cannot tell you.

  • Model scale is small: counted networks up to 1.4M parameters, CIR mixers up to 7M.

  • Hardware scope is one laptop CPU. No GPU results exist.

  • The English corpus is small, 3.86M tokens, so it repeats quickly.

  • Quality is bits per token on unseen contexts, in English and Indonesian.

  • Grammar, binding, reasoning and generation are not measurable at this scale.

  • Main results use two seeds. Per language and ladder results use one.

  • Decoding cost is unmeasured. Count tables need about 434 MB of memory.

  • Methods overlap with prior art, including modded nanogpt, DeltaNet, RWKV 7 and Engram.

  • Results can change as baselines improve. This has already happened several times.

  • CPU cost estimates may not transfer to GPU scale training.

16 · Publication and reproducibility

Enough to evaluate major claims independently.

DotrixAI intends to publish methodology, benchmark conditions, limitations and selected reproducibility materials. The aim is that major technical claims can be independently evaluated.

This is not a promise of full open source. Some implementation details will remain proprietary.

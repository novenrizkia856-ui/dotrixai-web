CIR

Researching lower cost to capability architectures and learning systems.

CIR is DotrixAI's first major research program. It compares candidate systems with strong Transformer baselines on total cost.

01 · Research objective

Reach the same capability for substantially less total cost.

minimize  TotalCost(CIR, Q) / TotalCost(Strong Transformer, Q)

Q is matched capability. Both systems must reach the same level before costs are compared.

The primary metric is cost to capability. FLOPs, parameters, BPB, tokens per second and wall clock speed are inputs, not the target.

Every new claim must also be compared with the cheapest fair Transformer we know. We report two denominators side by side.

What total cost can include

  • Training compute and wall clock time
  • Hardware utilization
  • Memory, activations and optimizer state
  • Data exposure
  • Infrastructure, energy and amortized hardware

Two denominators

  • Standard. A strong Transformer with fair, generic improvements.
  • Matched modules. The same Transformer given every generic module CIR uses.
  • The second isolates what is specific to CIR.

  • {{tagMeasured}}

    Read directly from an instrument, such as BPB or time per token.

  • {{tagEstimated}}

    Computed from measurements, such as a cost ratio at matched Q.

  • {{tagProjected}}

    Extrapolated beyond what was run. Never mixed with the other two.

02 · Research gates

Impact gates, not milestones.

Each gate is a cost ratio at matched capability. Status reflects only the latest evidence. A gate passed against a weaker baseline does not count against a stronger one.

03 · Current research state

What is being compared, and on what.

Implementation details beyond this level stay private while the research is active.

  • Strongest reproduced candidate

    A010. Two delta rule recurrent layers, then one softmax attention layer. Trained with Muon.

  • Strongest baseline

    B1A. A Transformer with a single global attention layer. Found by our own baseline attack.

  • Parameters

    About 3.94M effective. A second width reaches about 7M. {{tagMeasured}}

  • Context and batch

    4,096 tokens. Batch of 8 sequences, or 32,768 tokens per update.

  • Token budget

    1,500 updates, about 49M tokens. English and Indonesian text.

  • Hardware

    One commodity laptop CPU, Intel Core i5 13420H, two physical cores. No GPU yet.

  • Seeds

    Two seeds for the main A010 comparisons. Most other results use one seed.

  • Cost to Q against B1A

    BPB 1.23 to 1.27×. Associative recall 0.74 to 1.0×. {{tagEstimated}}

  • Cost to Q, earlier baselines

    0.59 to 0.64× in BPB, two seeds. {{tagEstimated}}

  • Capability evidence

    BPB, copying, associative recall and bracket closing. Grammar and reasoning are not yet measurable.

  • Scaling evidence

    Three widths at small scale. Nothing above about 7M parameters.

04 · Current claim

Current claim

Scope: about 4M parameters, 4,096 token context, one laptop CPU, up to 49M tokens.

In this small regime, A010 matched a gated local global Transformer's BPB. It needed 0.63 to 0.64× of that model's training cost. This was reproduced across two seeds.

Our own baseline attack then found a cheaper Transformer, B1A. It matches that baseline at about half the cost per token.

Against B1A, A010 needs about 1.23 to 1.27× the cost to reach the same BPB. At equal compute the two tie. A010's recall advantage shrinks to marginal.

CIR currently has no robust cost advantage over the cheapest fair Transformer we know. The program objective has not been reached.

06 · Cost to capability

A curve, not a headline number.

An advantage at one quality level can vanish at another. We report cost at several levels of Q.

TotalCostRatio(Q) ≈ ρ_u × ρ_c

  • ρ_u

    Relative updates, or tokens, needed to reach matched Q.

  • ρ_c

    Relative cost per update, measured on one canonical instrument.

  • A010 against B1A

    ρ_u about 0.70, ρ_c about 1.75 to 1.80. So the cost ratio is about 1.23 to 1.27. {{tagEstimated}}

The cheapest Transformer, B1A, is not on this chart. Its ratios come from a different cost session. Ratios are only compared within one valid session.

07 · Baseline adversary

We keep making the Transformer stronger.

A baseline lane acts as an adversary. Several CIR claims weakened or disappeared as a result.

  • Optimizer: Muon for both sides

    The pure recurrent advantage disappeared. The Transformer won by 0.051 BPB.

    Claim withdrawn

  • Gated blocks for the Transformer

    The strongest Transformer in quality. A010 still held at 0.59 to 0.63.

    Claim narrowed

  • Local global attention

    9% cheaper per token at nearly equal quality. Early Q ratios rose to 0.78 to 0.82.

    Claim narrowed

  • Learning rate screening for baselines

    An inherited baseline learning rate was flagged. Dependent claims are marked contested until attacked.

    Rule added

  • Engram memory module

    Helped the Transformer more than A010, closing about 63% of the margin.

    Claim weakened

  • Static n gram heads

    With the same heads on both sides, the recurrent specific edge fell to about zero.

    Claim weakened

08 · Reduced uncertainty

What We Have Ruled Out

Each item below closed a branch of the search. That makes the next experiment cheaper to choose.

Architecture

Pure recurrence under a strong optimizer

A delta rule model loses to a Muon trained Transformer at this scale.

Architecture

Longer context as the route to 0.50

Moving from 4,096 to 8,192 tokens made A010's per token ratio worse.

Architecture

Static lookup as recall

Lookup tables cannot perform in context associative recall. A softmax layer is still needed.

Architecture

Several hybrid variants

Local selection, a surprisal cache and a per head hybrid layer each failed their gates.

Architecture

Recurrent state replacing distant attention

When attention was windowed, A010 lost as much quality as the Transformer. Its state carried almost no distant copies.

Analysis

Mixer substitution reaching 0.50 here

Replace attention with a free recurrent core. The per token ratio still stays at 0.55 to 0.72. {{tagEstimated}}

Method

Ranking architectures at 250 updates

Rank correlation with final results was 0.09. Discovery runs now last 750 updates.

Method

Uncontrolled timing

Sessions with unpinned cores or background load are quarantined and remeasured.

Method

Endpoint cost for capability

Charging a plateaued baseline its full budget inflated CIR's advantage. It was corrected.

09 · Capability evaluation

Lower BPB is not general intelligence.

BPB measures text compression. Models with equal BPB can differ in recall, copying and state tracking. At 4M parameters, many capabilities cannot be measured at all.

{{cirCapabilities}}

Status compares A010 with the strongest relevant Transformer at the same scale. Probes are exploratory unless stated otherwise.

10 · Scaling status

Small scale results do not prove large scale behavior.

Three widths have been tested, all below 10M parameters. Evidence, extrapolation and hypothesis are kept apart.

Evidence {{tagEstimated}}

What was observed

Against the local global Transformer, the ratio rose from 0.64 to 0.72 at width 448. At a fixed tokens per parameter ratio, the BPB margin grew from 2.8M to 4M. Against B1A, ρ_u stayed at 0.70 and 0.71 across widths.

Confound

Tokens per parameter

The width 448 run kept tokens fixed. Tokens per parameter fell from 12.3 to 6.4. That alone could explain the weaker ratio.

Extrapolation {{tagProjected}}

Where attention cost goes

Attention's share of cost falls roughly with width. At width 4,096 and context 4,096, it is about 8%. Replacing attention then saves little.

Hypothesis

Where an asymmetry may remain

Recurrence keeps constant cost per token as context grows. That may matter only far beyond our tested context.

Unknown

Not yet tested

Anything above about 7M parameters. Training beyond 1,500 updates. GPUs and other CPUs. Longer contexts with real long range needs.

11 · Current threats to the claim

Why the remaining advantage could disappear.

Some of these threats have already materialized. They are listed in order of current weight.

  • 01Stronger Transformer baselines

    B1A already removed the BPB advantage. A windowed B1A may be 10 to 30% cheaper still.

    Materialized

  • 02Generic modules help Transformers too

    Engram and n gram heads gave Transformers equal or larger gains.

    Materialized

  • 03An analytic cost bound

    At this context length, swapping the mixer cannot reach the 0.50 gate.

    Estimated

  • 04Scaling degradation

    Attention's share of cost shrinks with width. Mixer savings shrink with it.

    Projected

  • 05A marginal capability edge

    The recall advantage against B1A is small and rests on one seed.

    Open

  • 06Hardware specificity

    One laptop CPU. Thin recurrent mixers are limited by call overhead there, not arithmetic.

    Open

  • 07Optimizer and tuning sensitivity

    Muon already reversed one result. Learning rate choices can still shift ratios.

    Open

  • 08Prior art

    Recurrent state tracking with negative eigenvalues appears in DeltaNet and RWKV 7 work.

    Acknowledged

14 · Methodology

How CIR evaluates a candidate.

Hypotheses

Hypothesis led, with credences

Each experiment tests a named hypothesis. Beliefs are updated before and after.

Criteria

Frozen success and failure criteria

Pass conditions and predictions are written down before any result is seen.

Funnel

Discovery separate from confirmation

Discovery runs are short. Confirmation needs a full run, a second seed and a baseline attack.

Baselines

Adversarial baseline strengthening

A dedicated lane tries to make the Transformer beat CIR.

Mechanism

Mechanism before optimization

Probes explain why a result happens before it is built upon.

Fidelity

Multi fidelity experiments

Analysis and evaluation only probes come first. Full training runs come last.

Cost

Canonical cost accounting

Fixed cores, calibration and validity checks. Invalid sessions are remeasured.

Capability

Capability probes beyond BPB

Copying, recall, bracket closing and position use are checked on the same checkpoints.

Search

First principles architecture search

Candidates are derived from bounds and primitives before literature is consulted.

Prior art

Adversarial prior art review

Every idea is searched against published work. Rediscoveries are labeled as such.

Red team

Periodic knowledge red teaming

Accepted conclusions are attacked on a schedule. Errors found are logged and corrected.

Status

Epistemic labels

Knowledge carries a status, such as supported, contested, superseded or quarantined.

15 · Limitations

What these results cannot tell you.

  • Model scale is small: about 4M parameters, at most about 7M.

  • Hardware scope is one laptop CPU. No GPU results exist.

  • Training is short: at most 49M tokens, below compute optimal budgets.

  • Quality is mostly BPB on a small English and Indonesian corpus.

  • Grammar, binding, reasoning and generation are not measurable at this scale.

  • Most results use one seed. Only the main A010 comparisons use two.

  • Mechanisms overlap with prior art, including DeltaNet, RWKV 7 and Engram.

  • Results can change as baselines improve. This has already happened several times.

  • CPU cost estimates may not transfer to GPU scale training.

16 · Publication and reproducibility

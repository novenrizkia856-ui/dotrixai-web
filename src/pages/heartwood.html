Main research focus

Heartwood

Learning that builds on what came before.

How can models learn new domains while retaining earlier capabilities? Heartwood investigates continued learning, model growth and the cost of useful adaptation.

Program overview

Keep learning. Keep earlier capabilities.

Models encounter new information and changing tasks. Learning something new can weaken abilities learned earlier.

Heartwood asks how existing knowledge can support later learning. Model capacity may also need to grow as the work changes.

The goal combines retention, adaptation and efficient use of computation. Saving compute alone is not enough.

What the research asks

Retention

Which earlier capabilities survive after a model learns something new?

Adaptation

Can the model learn new domains well, rather than only preserving old ones?

Knowledge reuse

Does earlier learning make later learning easier?

Growth

When does added capacity help, and when does it add unnecessary cost?

Fair comparisons

How does continued learning compare with strong alternatives at comparable quality?

Practical cost

What does the full learning process cost, rather than one isolated step?

Evidence and limits

A research question, not a universal claim.

Work is being evaluated through controlled internal experiments. Comparisons examine old and new capabilities together.

Small experimental models cannot establish results for every architecture or scale. Broad claims about eliminating forgetting remain unproven.

This page presents the program's scope and current status. Experimental results, implementations and training recipes remain unreleased.

Future technical claims will need evidence with clear baselines and limitations.
